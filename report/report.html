Urdu BPE Tokenizer from Scratch

1. Overview

This report describes a byte-level BPE tokenizer written without a tokenizer library. It was trained on a new Urdu corpus assembled for this submission. The corpus uses Urdu Wikipedia, Urdu Wikisource and Urdu mC4, so it does not reuse Daily Express, Daily Jang, BBC Urdu, Makhzan or CC-100 from the supplied solution. The final vocabulary contains exactly 50,000 token IDs.

Design choiceImplementation
Base vocabularyIDs 0-255 are raw byte values. This makes every Unicode string lossless and removes the need for an unknown token.
Special token<|endoftext|> has ID 256 and never enters pair counting or merging.
Learned merges49,743 ordered merges occupy IDs 257-49,999. A SHA-256 checksum protects their order in tokenizer.json.
Tie-breakingThe pair with greatest frequency is selected; tied pairs use the lexicographically greater integer pair.
Pre-tokenizationThe Urdu-aware regex keeps combining marks and internal ZWNJ inside a word and attaches one leading space to the next unit.
TrainingThe reference trainer recounts all pairs. The fast trainer uses weighted unique types, a pair-to-word index, local count updates and an encoding cache.
NormalizationNFC normalization maps Arabic yeh, kaf and heh variants, uses ASCII digits, removes tatweel and controls, and preserves meaningful Urdu heh forms.

2. Corpus and Training Setup

The clean corpus contains 609.05 MiB, 129,770 documents, 74,627,570 words and 857,782 unique word forms. The two Wikimedia collectors produced 118.34 MiB of raw self-collected text with per-page provenance records.

Cleaning removes wiki and HTML markup, URLs, email addresses, phone-like strings and IP addresses. It applies the idempotent normalizer, filters low-Urdu lines, removes lines found in at least 50 documents, and removes exact normalized duplicates by SHA-256. A source-stratified shuffle with seed 4,172,026 produced a 98/1/1 document split. The three split hash sets are pairwise disjoint.

The first attested space-prefixed whole Urdu word is ␠کی at merge 32 (ID 288). Earlier merges mainly reconstruct individual letters and space-plus-lead-byte fragments.

There are 106 learned tokens that are invalid UTF-8 alone. They are legal internal byte fragments and can be completed by later merges. Examples: #3 20 d8 · #5 20 d9 · #12 d8 a7 d8 · #35 20 d8 a7 d8 · #93 e2 80 · #435 20 e2 80.

The progression is consistent with byte BPE. Frequent letter bytes are joined first, followed by common suffixes, function words and longer domain-specific strings. By merge 5,000, several regular Urdu words and names are already represented by single tokens.

5. Question 3: Inputs the Tokenizer Handles Badly

Case 1: Roman Urdu with shortened spellings

yr plzz mjhy bta do assignment kb submit krni hy???

Ours: 2.50 tokens/word; o200k_base: 1.50; GPT-2: 1.70. Training is overwhelmingly Perso-Arabic, so shortened Latin spellings and repeated punctuation fall back to small byte pieces.

Pieces: yr | ␠pl | z | z | ␠m | j | hy | ␠b | ta | ␠do | ␠as | s

Case 2: Heavily vowelled Arabic-style text

قُرْآنِ حَکِیْمْ کِی تِلَاوَتْ بِالتَّجْوِیْدِ

Ours: 3.80 tokens/word; o200k_base: 6.80; GPT-2: 11.00. The regex correctly keeps marks with the word, but rare fully-vowelled combinations did not occur often enough to earn long merges.

Pieces: ق | ُرْآن | ِ | ␠حَک | ِیْم | ْ | ␠ک | ِی | ␠ت | ِلَ | او | َتْ

Case 3: Mixed technical identifier

ماڈل ID XQ-97_v2.4 کا SHA256=9f86d081884c7d65 ہے۔

Ours: 1.45 tokens/word; o200k_base: 1.40; GPT-2: 2.00. Mixed identifiers are intentionally lossless, yet uncommon letter-digit-symbol sequences remain fragmented.

Pieces: ماڈل | ␠ID | ␠X | Q | - | 97 | _ | v | 2 | . | 4 | ␠کا
